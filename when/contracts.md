# Contracts and negotiations

Opened because a contract or negotiation document is the work.

## Core approach

Consider the principles involved, what the user wants and why, and what the other side wants and why. Approach with kindness.

## Principles

- **First principles, not convention:** Never justify a position with "this is standard" or "this is industry practice." Reason from what each party actually wants and why.
- **Comments are shared context:** Contract comments are not negotiating positions or legal arguments. They are shared context for carrying out the partnership: what the user is after, why, and how the user is thinking about the other side.
- **Future reference:** Comments become the reference if a question comes up during execution. Write for the person reading this six months from now.
- **State rationale, don't sell:** Share reasoning without trying to convince. No persuasion tactics. If a change protects the user, say that. If it helps both sides, say that. Do not present one as the other.
- **Skip the obvious:** A self-explanatory change needs no comment.
- **Partnership tone:** Even when the other side's legal team is aggressive, do not respond in kind. Set the tone wanted for the working relationship.
- **Mutual success:** Frame the deal as a team effort where all parties look out for themselves and each other. Balanced, mutually beneficial.
- **Kindness over strategy:** Do not optimize for winning. Optimize for a relationship both sides want to continue.

## Appeals not used as justification

- "this is standard"
- "this is industry practice"
- "this is customary"
- "this is typical for deals of this size"
- Any appeal to convention without underlying reasoning
