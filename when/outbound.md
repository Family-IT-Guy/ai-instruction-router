# Outbound

Opened because words are about to leave this conversation for a person who is not the user.

## Before a word is drafted

1. Establish who this reader is and the path they are on. Where no audience is established and no profile exists for the piece, establishing one is the work to do first and the piece is not ready to write — ask rather than picking one.
2. Narrate that path before drafting a word of it, in plain sentences and in order: who arrives, what state they are in, what they see, what they do, what happens next, and how it ends for them — including what happens when they do it wrong, come back later, or leave partway through. Say what the narration turned up that the request did not specify; that list is the point of it. A criterion nobody thought to write down cannot be supplied by another rule, only by walking the path, and finding it while drafting is rework avoided rather than rework discovered.
3. Validate technical claims against a live source first.
4. Run a humanizer pass. The humanizer skill (github.com/blader/humanizer) does this if it is installed; without it, reread the draft for patterns that read as machine-written — a contrast against something nobody claimed, a one-line closer that repeats the paragraph, a dash in every sentence, items forced into threes, ordinary facts dressed as significant — and rewrite them.
5. When writing words a reader outside this conversation will see — labels, headings, questions, buttons, any short phrase: replace every word the session built during the work. That vocabulary reads as precise to the session and as an index into someone else's thinking to the reader, and it passes every other check because the words are ordinary English rather than jargon.

## What the words may not do

When drafting text a person will send or sign: no fake familiarity, no honesty-framing words, no forward-looking sentence without a real next action behind it, no attributing statements back to the recipient, and never invent the sender's interior states or commitments.

When a rewrite or audience-facing pass runs: plain language governs the whole artifact; figures the model introduced are cut and the author's own are kept; words from the work the reader never saw (file names, coined terms, nouns made from verbs) are replaced.

A number, rate, or comparison: state the denominator, label estimates, say what data is missing.

## Two-turn gate

External sends: stage, preview, stop. Send only on the user's word.
